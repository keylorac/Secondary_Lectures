Uniformly Accelerated Motion

Every situation, one toolkit.

In MUA the acceleration $a$ is constant. The sign of $a$ and the starting speed decide the situation. We study four:
A Starts from rest
$v_i=0$, speeding up
B Speeds up
$v_i>0$, $a>0$
C Slows down or brakes
$a<0$, sometimes $v_f=0$
D Vertical motion
free fall and throwing up, $|a|=g$

Each situation has a lab, a solved example and a hint. We finish with a mixed practice set.

The Toolkit: Four Equations

$$a=\frac{v_f-v_i}{t}\quad(1)$$
$$d=\frac{(v_f+v_i)\,t}{2}\quad(2)$$
$$d=\frac{v_f^2-v_i^2}{2a}\quad(3)$$
$$d=v_i\,t+\frac{a\,t^2}{2}\quad(4)$$

$d$ distance (m) · $v_i$ initial speed (m/s) · $v_f$ final speed (m/s) · $a$ acceleration (m/s²) · $t$ time (s)

Which equation? Each problem has 5 variables. Three are known, one is asked, and one is absent. Pick the absent variable:
Absent $d$: use $a=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{t}$, equation (1).
Absent $a$: use $d=\dfrac{(v_f+v_i)t}{2}$, equation (2).
Absent $t$: use $d=\dfrac{v_f^2-v_i^2}{2a}$, equation (3).
Absent $v_f$: use $d=v_it+\dfrac{at^2}{2}$, equation (4).
Sign rule. Choose a positive direction. If the object speeds up, $a$ has the sign of $v$. If it slows down, $a$ is negative when $v$ is positive.

MUA Lab

$v_i$ m/s   $a$ m/s²   $T$ s

Dots mark each second. The shaded area under the $v$–$t$ graph is the distance. For a trapezoid that area is $\frac{(v_f+v_i)\,t}{2}$, which is equation (2).

A Starts From Rest

Here $v_i=0$. The equations simplify: $v_f=a\,t$ and $d=\dfrac{a\,t^2}{2}$. Distance grows with $t^2$: double the time, four times the distance.
Example. A motorbike starts from rest and reaches 20 m/s in 5 s. Find $a$ and the distance.
Given: $v_i=0$, $v_f=20$, $t=5$. Unknown: $a$, $d$.
Acceleration (1): $a=\dfrac{20-0}{5}=4\ \text{m/s}^2$
Distance (2): $d=\dfrac{(20+0)(5)}{2}=50\ \text{m}$
Check (4): $d=0+\dfrac{(4)(25)}{2}=50\ \text{m}$ ✓

B Speeds Up From a Moving Start

Here $v_i>0$ and $a>0$. The object is already moving and gains speed every second.
Example 1: time is given. A bus at 10 m/s accelerates at 1.5 m/s² for 6 s. Find $v_f$ and $d$.
Given: $v_i=10$, $a=1.5$, $t=6$.
From (1): $v_f=v_i+at=10+(1.5)(6)=19\ \text{m/s}$
From (2): $d=\dfrac{(19+10)(6)}{2}=87\ \text{m}$
Check (4): $d=(10)(6)+\dfrac{(1.5)(36)}{2}=60+27=87\ \text{m}$ ✓
Example 2: time is absent. A car at 6 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s² while covering 40 m. Find $v_f$ and $t$.
Absent $t$: use (3): $v_f^2=v_i^2+2ad=36+2(2)(40)=196$
Answer: $v_f=14\ \text{m/s}$
Time from (1): $t=\dfrac{14-6}{2}=4\ \text{s}$
Check (2): $d=\dfrac{(14+6)(4)}{2}=40\ \text{m}$ ✓

C Slowing Down and Braking

Here $a<0$. The velocity and the acceleration point in opposite directions. If the object stops, then $v_f=0$. Keep the minus sign: it is part of the answer.
Example 1: braking to a stop. A car at 20 m/s brakes at $-4$ m/s² until it stops. Find the time and stopping distance.
Given: $v_i=20$, $v_f=0$, $a=-4$.
Time (1): $t=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{a}=\dfrac{0-20}{-4}=5\ \text{s}$
Distance (3): $d=\dfrac{0-400}{2(-4)}=50\ \text{m}$
Check (2): $d=\dfrac{(0+20)(5)}{2}=50\ \text{m}$ ✓
Example 2: slowing without stopping. A cyclist slows from 12 m/s to 4 m/s in 4 s. Find $a$ and $d$.
(1): $a=\dfrac{4-12}{4}=-2\ \text{m/s}^2$
(2): $d=\dfrac{(4+12)(4)}{2}=32\ \text{m}$
Check (4): $d=(12)(4)+\dfrac{(-2)(16)}{2}=48-16=32\ \text{m}$ ✓

D Vertical Motion

Gravity gives a constant acceleration of about $g=10\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward (we round 9.8). Choose one positive direction and keep it. If down is positive, then $a=+10$. If up is positive, then $a=-10$.
Free fall: $v_i=0$. Thrown up: at the top $v=0$, and the ball returns to the start with the same speed.
Example 1: free fall. A ball is dropped and hits the ground after 2 s. Find the height and impact speed. (Down is positive here.)
Given: $v_i=0$, $a=10$, $t=2$.
Height (4): $d=0+\dfrac{(10)(4)}{2}=20\ \text{m}$
Speed (1): $v_f=0+(10)(2)=20\ \text{m/s}$
Example 2: thrown up. A ball is thrown up at 20 m/s. Find the time to the top and the maximum height. (Up is positive: $a=-10$.)
At the top: $v_f=0$. Given $v_i=20$, $a=-10$.
Time (1): $t=\dfrac{0-20}{-10}=2\ \text{s}$
Height (3): $d=\dfrac{0-400}{2(-10)}=20\ \text{m}$
Whole flight: up 2 s and down 2 s, so 4 s. It lands at 20 m/s. ✓

Mixed Practice

Name the situation (A, B, C or D). Then list Given, Unknown and the absent variable, and choose the equation. Use $g=10$ m/s². Type the answer and check it.
