1. Concept of Force and Net Force

A force ($\vec{F}$) is a vector quantity representing an interaction capable of altering the state of motion or shape of a body.

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum \vec{F}$$
$\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$: resultant or net force $[\text{N}]$ · The Newton (N) is the standard unit of force: $1\text{ N} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$.
Topic 1 Example — Net Force Calculation Step 0 / 4
A crate is pushed to the right with a force $F_1 = 45\text{ N}$ and pulled to the left by a friction force $F_2 = 15\text{ N}$. Determine the magnitude and direction of the net force ($\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$).
Step 1 — Define Vector Directions
Assign horizontal signs: right is positive ($+$), left is negative ($-$).
$F_1 = +45\text{ N} \quad \text{and} \quad F_2 = -15\text{ N}$
Step 2 — Apply the Vector Sum Formula
$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum F = F_1 + F_2 = (+45\text{ N}) + (-15\text{ N})$$
Step 3 — Compute Resultant
$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 30\text{ N}$$
Final Result
The net force is $30\text{ N}$ pointing to the right.

2. Newton's First Law (Inertia)
Law of Inertia: Every body continues in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a straight line, unless acted upon by net external forces.

If the sum of all forces is zero, acceleration is zero (translational equilibrium):

$$\sum \vec{F} = 0 \iff \vec{a} = 0$$
Topic 2 Example — Tension in Translational Equilibrium Step 0 / 4
A traffic light weighing $200\text{ N}$ hangs in equilibrium from two symmetrical cables attached to a ceiling. Each cable makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the horizontal. Calculate the tension $T$ in each cable.
Step 1 — Equilibrium Conditions
Since the object is motionless, $\sum F_x = 0$ and $\sum F_y = 0$. By symmetry, both cable tensions are equal ($T_1 = T_2 = T$).
Step 2 — Vertical Forces Equilibrium Equation
$$\sum F_y = T \sin(60^\circ) + T \sin(60^\circ) - W = 0$$ $$2T \sin(60^\circ) = 200\text{ N}$$
Step 3 — Solve for Cable Tension ($T$)
$$T = \frac{200\text{ N}}{2 \cdot \sin(60^\circ)} = \frac{200}{2 \cdot 0.866} = 115.47\text{ N}$$
Final Result
The tension in each cable is $115.47\text{ N}$.

3. Newton's Second Law (Dynamics)
Fundamental Law of Dynamics: The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it, and inversely proportional to its mass.
$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = m \cdot \vec{a} \implies \vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}_{\text{net}}}{m}$$

Topic 3 Example — Acceleration of a Shopping Cart Step 0 / 4
A student exerts a horizontal net force of $80\text{ N}$ on a $25\text{ kg}$ cart. Calculate the cart's acceleration and the velocity reached after $4\text{ s}$ starting from rest.
Step 1 — Identify Given Variables
$F_{\text{net}} = 80\text{ N} \quad m = 25\text{ kg} \quad v_0 = 0\text{ m/s} \quad t = 4\text{ s}$
Step 2 — Calculate Acceleration ($a$)
$$a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \frac{80\text{ N}}{25\text{ kg}} = 3.20\text{ m/s}^2$$
Step 3 — Calculate Final Velocity ($v$)
$$v = v_0 + a \cdot t = 0 + (3.20\text{ m/s}^2)(4\text{ s}) = 12.80\text{ m/s}$$
Final Result
Acceleration is $3.20\text{ m/s}^2$ and final velocity is $12.80\text{ m/s}$.

4. Inclined Plane and Vector Decomposition

When an object rests or moves on an inclined surface, its weight ($\vec{w}$) must be resolved into two components relative to the surface axes:

$$w_x = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta)$$ $$w_y = m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

Topic 4 Example — Acceleration on an Inclined Ramp Step 0 / 4
A $15\text{ kg}$ block slides down a frictionless ramp inclined at $40^\circ$ above the horizontal. Calculate the weight components ($w_x, w_y$), the normal force $N$, and the magnitude of acceleration ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
Step 1 — Calculate Total Weight ($w$)
$$w = m \cdot g = (15\text{ kg})(9.8\text{ m/s}^2) = 147\text{ N}$$
Step 2 — Decompose Weight Components
$$w_x = w \cdot \sin(40^\circ) = 147 \cdot 0.6428 = 94.49\text{ N} \quad (\text{parallel to ramp})$$ $$w_y = w \cdot \cos(40^\circ) = 147 \cdot 0.7660 = 112.61\text{ N} \quad (\text{perpendicular to ramp})$$
Step 3 — Determine Normal Force & Acceleration
Since there is no vertical movement off the ramp, $N = w_y = 112.61\text{ N}$.
Applying Newton's 2nd Law down the ramp: $$a = \frac{w_x}{m} = \frac{94.49\text{ N}}{15\text{ kg}} = 6.30\text{ m/s}^2$$
Final Result
Normal force is $112.61\text{ N}$ and downhill acceleration is $6.30\text{ m/s}^2$.

5. Newton's Third Law (Action-Reaction)
Law of Action and Reaction: For every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction; the mutual actions of two bodies upon each other are always equal and directed to contrary parts.
$$\vec{F}_{A \to B} = -\vec{F}_{B \to A}$$
Remember that action and reaction forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other out in the free-body diagram of a single object.
Topic 5 Example — Model Rocket Propulsion Step 0 / 4
A model rocket engine pushes exhaust gases downward with a force of $120\text{ N}$. Determine the thrust force exerted on the $2\text{ kg}$ rocket. Calculate the initial upward acceleration ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
Step 1 — Identify the Action-Reaction Pair
By Newton's Third Law, if the rocket pushes gases downward with $120\text{ N}$, the gases exert an equal reaction thrust of $120\text{ N}$ upward on the rocket.
Step 2 — Calculate Gravity/Weight Force ($w$)
$$w = m \cdot g = (2\text{ kg})(9.8\text{ m/s}^2) = 19.6\text{ N} \quad (\text{downward})$$
Step 3 — Compute Net Force & Upward Acceleration
$$F_{\text{net}} = F_{\text{thrust}} - w = 120\text{ N} - 19.6\text{ N} = 100.4\text{ N}$$ $$a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \frac{100.4\text{ N}}{2\text{ kg}} = 50.20\text{ m/s}^2$$
Final Result
Upward thrust is $120\text{ N}$, giving an initial acceleration of $50.20\text{ m/s}^2$.
